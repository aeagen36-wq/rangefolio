# Drill Provenance, Procedure and Benchmark Research

Research date: 2026-09-24. Scope: verify origin, procedure, published tiers and scoring conventions for the drills the app will time and grade. Only originator pages, USPSA classifier documents, Gunsite/NRA-affiliated publications, and instructor curricula with published numbers were used. Where the only source is a secondary write-up, that is stated. Anything not verifiable from a trusted source is marked **UNVERIFIED** rather than filled in.

Conventions used below:
- "Originator-stated" = the drill's author's own page or a page on their organization's site.
- "Recognized authority" = Gunsite, USPSA, Rangemaster, KR Training, pistol-training.com, Ben Stoeger, Gabe White, Kyle Defoor, Sage Dynamics, Larry Vickers (for Hackathorn drills).
- "Inference" = my reading, not a sourced claim. Marked explicitly.

## 1. Bill Drill (7 yd, 6 rounds)

**Origin**
- Created by Bill Wilson (Wilson Combat / IDPA co-founder). Rob Leatham is credited with coining the name "Bill Drill" (AmmoLand, secondary). The KR Training drill compilation lists originator as Bill Wilson.
- **UNVERIFIED**: I could not locate a Bill Wilson / Wilson Combat primary web page describing the drill; wilsoncombat.com site search returned only product pages. Every procedure/standard below is from secondary sources that attribute the numbers to Wilson, or from other authorities' versions.

**Procedure (as commonly published; attributed to Wilson)**
- Target: IPSC/USPSA silhouette (A-zone) or IDPA target (-0 / 8" circle).
- Distance: 7 yards.
- Start: holstered, hands relaxed at sides (start position "discretionary" per AmmoLand).
- On signal: draw and fire 6 rounds.
- Hit standard: all 6 in the A-zone / -0.

**Published standards**
- Bill Wilson (as quoted in KR Training compilation and AmmoLand): "3 seconds from open carry as a goal for a good shooter" / "three seconds with carry equipment."
- KR Training compilation also lists: 2 seconds from competition gear (attributed to Brian Enos); 5 seconds as a "recommended standard" for defensive students.
- Ben Stoeger (originator-level authority for competition): goal times 3 yd 1.7 s, 5 yd 1.8 s, 7 yd 2.0 s; "At closer ranges, you should strive for all 'A' zone hits. At longer ranges, the occasional close 'C' is no big deal."
- Gabe White Technical Skills Test (7 yd, from holster, USPSA/IDPA target): Turbo pin 2.00 s, Light pin 2.50 s, Dark pin 3.50 s; C/-1 hits +0.25 s, D +1.0 s, miss +2.0 s; 0.25 s bonus if from concealment or active-retention duty holster; pin = standard met 4 of 8 attempts.
- Kyle Defoor Pistol Test 5 (USPSA target, from holster): 6 rounds at 5 yd < 3.0 s, 10 yd < 3.5 s, 15 yd < 4.0 s, 20 yd < 4.5 s; "20 Alphas to pass and no Deltas" (24 rounds total).
- KR Training (Karl Rehn) working goals: 7 yd "3 seconds all A's"; 15 yd Bill Drill "6 seconds all A's."
- USPSA classifier: "08-01 4 Bill Drill" (retired) and "24-04 The Thrill of the Bill Drill" (active). 24-04 recommended HHFs (2025 Classifier Committee doc): Open 10.2616, Limited 9.1569, Production 9.5393, Revolver 7.4871, Single Stack 9.1730, Carry Optics 9.4176, PCC 11.5200, Limited Optics 9.4176 (prior HHFs: Open 12.8598, Lim 11.3568, Prod 10.6235, Rev 7.9594, SS 10.2326, CO 12.2000, PCC 14.3542, LO 12.2474). **UNVERIFIED**: uspsa.org/viewer/24-04.pdf is blocked to fetch, so I could not confirm 24-04's exact course of fire or point total and therefore cannot convert HHF to a time.

**What it trains (as stated)**
- Stoeger: at close range "a fast draw with correct grip, and staying relaxed"; at longer distance "a trigger control test"; recommends the Doubles drill before Bill Drills; grip-pressure experimentation (lighter strong hand for trigger speed, firmer support hand for control).
- Safariland article (secondary): drawstroke, sight tracking through recoil, recoil control, trigger control.
- Gabe White: pins measure "core technical skills of drawing and shooting" — grip, trigger control, aiming-system use, draw consistency.

**Scoring conventions**
- All-A pass/fail (AmmoLand: any miss = fail regardless of time); Rehn "all A's"; Defoor point count with Delta = fail.
- Time-plus: Gabe White +0.25/+1.0/+2.0; Safariland-described convention +1 s per non-A.
- Hit factor when run as USPSA 24-04.

## 2. El Presidente

**Origin**
- Jeff Cooper, created while training a South American presidential security detail; published to the public in the 1970s via American Handgunner (Safariland/Gunsite-affiliated article). Shooting Illustrated (NRA): Cooper built it "as a means to check shooter proficiency"; the name "El Presidente" came later when IPSC adopted it as a standard stage.

**Cooper's procedure (Shooting Illustrated, quoting Gunsite's Ed Head)**
- Three Gunsite silhouettes, 10 yards, spaced 1 yard apart.
- Start: back to targets, holstered (surrender position common).
- On signal: turn, draw, two rounds each target, reload, two rounds each target. 12 rounds.
- Par: 10 s (semi-auto), 12 s (revolver).
- Cooper scoring: 5 pts per hit in the 8" center ring, 2 pts other hits, 60 possible; +5 for every full second under 10, -5 for every full second over 10.
- Cooper's expert benchmark (per Ed Head): "anyone capable of performing this drill on demand, with a suitable carry pistol, achieving a score of 45 or better, was probably an expert with their firearm and carry gear."

**USPSA classifier CM 99-11 (official course)**
- Start: standing in Box A (3 ft x 3 ft), back to targets, toes against rear fault line, wrists above shoulders. Three metric targets. Draw, two rounds each, mandatory reload, two rounds each. 12 rounds, Virginia Count, 60 points. Failure to reload = one procedural per shot fired.
- HHFs, 2025 Classifier Committee recommendations (prior value in parentheses): Open 12.1268 (13.5751); Limited 10.5552 (12.4345); Production 10.1008 (11.6423); Revolver 7.7012 (9.1836); Single Stack 9.9873 (11.3537); Carry Optics 10.3391 (11.6423); PCC 12.6276 (14.0093); Limited Optics 10.5956 (12.0965).
- Implied 100% (GM-level) all-A time = 60 / HHF: Production 5.94 s, CO 5.80 s, Limited 5.68 s, Open 4.95 s, PCC 4.75 s (recommended values); using prior HHFs: Production 5.15 s, Open 4.42 s. Class brackets unchanged: 95% GM, 85% M, 75% A, 60% B, 40% C (so Production M ≈ 6.99 s, A ≈ 7.92 s, B ≈ 9.90 s at 60 points).
- **Flag**: the 2025 document is a "recommended" HHF list; I could not confirm from uspsa.org which value set is currently in force.

**Other published standards**
- Hackathorn Standards string 8: El Presidente at 10 yd, 10 s par, 6 rounds loaded (forces reload).
- Steve Anderson (dry-fire author) via RecoilSports interview: students get "El Prez's down to 4 seconds."

**What it trains (as stated)**
- Cooper/Gunsite: overall proficiency check. Stated components not itemized by Cooper in the sources; **inference**: turn, draw, transitions, reload, cadence.

**Scoring conventions**
- Cooper: points +/- time. USPSA: hit factor (points / time), Virginia count. Hackathorn: par time pass/fail with point values.

## 3. Mozambique / Failure (to Stop) Drill

**Origin**
- Mike Rousseau (Rhodesian mercenary) incident at Lourenço Marques airport during the Mozambican War of Independence; two Hi-Power rounds to the chest failed to stop, third shot to the head/neck did. Cooper named it "the Mozambique" and added it to Gunsite's curriculum in the late 1970s. Renamed "Failure Drill" in 1980 by LAPD SWAT officers Larry Mudgett and John Helms (Wikipedia citing Shooting Illustrated and The Armory Life).

**Procedure**
- Two shots center chest, assess, one shot to the head ("between the eyebrows and the upper lip") — Shooting Illustrated (Jim Wilson). Cooper's own distance/par was not stated in any trusted source found. **UNVERIFIED**: original Gunsite distance and par.

**Published standards**
- Safariland (Gunsite-affiliated article): "A good goal is around 1.5 seconds without a draw and an additional second if you start from the holster" (i.e., ~2.5 s from holster).
- Gabe White "Failure to Stop" (7 yd, holster, 2 body + 1 head into 4" circle or 3x5 card): Turbo 1.70 s, Light 2.25 s, Dark 2.90 s.
- Hackathorn Half Cup drill, stage 3: 5 yd, 2 body + 1 head, 3.0 s.
- KR Training compilation: "Failure Drill" 7 yd, 5 strings of 2+1, "typically 2 seconds per string."
- Gunsite 250 school drill does not contain a 2+1 string (see positional section).

**What it trains (as stated)**
- Gunsite/Shooting Illustrated: follow-through and assessment after the pair; precise head shot when the body shots fail.
- Gabe White: draw, grip, trigger control, aiming system use.
- **Inference**: transition from a large to a small zone (cadence change).

**Scoring conventions**
- Pass/fail all hits within par; time-plus (Gabe White scheme).

## 4. F.A.S.T. (Fundamentals, Accuracy, & Speed Test)

**Origin**: Todd Louis Green, pistol-training.com.

**Originator's procedure**
- 7 yards. Target: 3x5" card (head) and 8" circle/plate (body).
- Start: "weapon concealed or in duty condition with all holster retention devices active; shooter facing downrange in relaxed stance with arms down at sides." Gun loaded with 1 in chamber + 1 in magazine (sets up slide-lock reload).
- On signal: draw, 2 rounds to the 3x5, slide-lock reload, 4 rounds to the 8" circle. 6 rounds.
- Adjustments: retention duty holster + covered mag pouches subtract 0.5 s; open-top retention holster with no concealment add 0.5 s.
- Penalties (per Shooting Wire summary of Green's rules): all hits must be in the zones; Green's published rule adds 2 s per miss on the head and 1 s per miss on the body. **Flag**: the penalty values were not returned verbatim by the fetch of pistol-training.com; treat as commonly published but re-check against the page.

**Originator's tiers**
- 10+ s Novice; < 10 s Intermediate; < 7 s Advanced; < 5 s Expert.
- Coin: "A sub 5 second clean run, twice in a row, is required to win a F.A.S.T. Coin," only in official pistol-training.com classes/recognized events. 27 numbered coins awarded as of the page (#00 Todd Green; #26 March 2024).
- Class Top Shooters list (as printed): Gabe White 4.58, Jimmy Creed 4.59, Mike Seeklander 4.80, Spencer Keepers 4.83, CCT125US 4.84.
- Shooting Wire reports "The world record, according to Todd's FASTest page is 3.56 seconds posted by Dave Sevigny." The current pistol-training.com page fetched did not show that entry; **flag** as historically reported, not currently displayed.

**What it trains (as stated)**: "Fundamentals, Accuracy, & Speed" — draw from concealment, precision pair, slide-lock reload, fast body string. (Component list is the acronym plus the procedure; Green's page did not itemize further in fetched text.)

**Scoring**: time-plus penalties; tiers by total time; coin requires clean runs.

## 5. Draw to first shot (7 yd and 15 yd)

No single "originator" drill; benchmarks drawn from published tests:
- Ben Stoeger, Far Target Standards (25 yd, USPSA target): Draw 1 round — Beginner 2.0 s, Intermediate 1.5 s, Advanced 1.1 s.
- Gunsite 250 school drill: 7 yd, draw and 2 body shots in 1.5 s; 10 yd in 2.0 s; 3 yd head shot in 1.5 s (Shooting Illustrated).
- Gabe White: Bill Drill Turbo 2.00 s for 6 rounds at 7 yd (draw plus five splits — **inference**: implies ≈1.0 s draw with ≈0.20 s splits).
- Kyle Defoor Test 1: 25 yd, concealed draw + 2 rounds under 2.5 s, x3, 25 points to pass (A=5, C=3, D=1).
- Steve Anderson: "all of his students get sub-second draws" (RecoilSports interview).
- American Pistol Institute (Gunsite) 1980 Basic Drill dry-fire program: "draw goal 1 second" (KR compilation).
- Rangemaster Baseline Assessment string 1: 5 yd, draw and 5 rounds in 5 s (B-8).
- KR Training: "draw and reload times ... result in the biggest gains ... draw time can be as much as half of the total string time."
- **UNVERIFIED**: a published 15-yd-specific draw tier from any originator on the trusted list. Interpolate between Gunsite 10 yd/2.0 s (2 shots) and Stoeger 25 yd/1.1 s advanced (1 shot) and mark as app-defined.

## 6. Concealed draw

- pistol-training.com F.A.S.T.: concealment is the baseline condition; open-top no-concealment adds 0.5 s (see §4).
- Gabe White: 0.25 s bonus for concealment or active-retention duty holster on all four tests.
- Defoor Tests 1–3: "concealed draw" required (25 yd, 2 rds < 2.5 s; 10 rds on B-8 < 10 s scoring 90).
- KR Training Three Seconds or Less: strings 1 and 3 are from concealment at 3 yd, 3 body shots in 3 s.
- AmmoLand (secondary) commonly quoted Bill Drill benchmark "3.5 seconds from concealment"; Wilson's "3 seconds with carry equipment."
- **Inference** for app: apply +0.5 s (Green) or +0.25 s (White) offsets when comparing concealed vs open-carry runs; both are published by the respective originators.

## 7. Speed reload (1-reload-1) and Four Aces (2-reload-2)

**1-reload-1 / 1-reload-2**
- Gunsite "One Plus One Pistol Reload Drill" (Freddie Blish, gunsite.com, 2021): 4" circle at 10 yd; draw, 1 round, speed reload, 1 round, x5 (10 rounds). "Goal is 5 seconds, Objective is 7 seconds" (per 5-run aggregate as written).
- pistol-training.com "1-Reload-2": 5 yd, gun aimed in on target, fire 1, reload, fire 2; torso zone (A-zone / -0 / 8" plate / 8.5x11). No par published. Stated purpose: "get a good grip and visual awareness of the pistol after the reload in order to control the gun and get two accurate and fast hits."
- Dot Torture dots 9–10: draw, 1 shot on #9, speed reload, 1 shot on #10, x3 (see §10).
- **UNVERIFIED**: a Todd Green "1-R-1" par time; none found on pistol-training.com.

**Four Aces**
- Origin: **UNVERIFIED**. Widely run; Ben Stoeger publishes it as "Double Reload Double (Four Aces)" but does not claim authorship.
- Stoeger procedure: "Draw and fire two rounds into the A zone, then reload, then fire two more rounds." Goal times: 3 yd 2.2 s, 5 yd 2.3 s, 7 yd 2.5 s. "Gives you a good idea of what you can do in terms of draw speed and reload speed." Diagnostic: "If you find yourself unable to make that 2.5 second goal time, carefully go through the data from the timer and find the 'low hanging fruit'." Notes competitive reloads ≈1 s.
- KR Training goal: 7 yd "5 seconds all A's" (defensive-student standard); student range 3.39–7.05 s.
- Stoeger Far Target Standards (25 yd): draw, 2, reload, 2 — Beginner 5.0 s, Intermediate 4.0 s, Advanced 3.5 s.

**Reload benchmarks in other tests**
- Rangemaster Baseline string 4: 7 yd, 3 rounds, empty-gun reload, 3 rounds, 10 s.
- Rangemaster Bullseye Course: 7 yd, 5 + slide-lock reload + 5, 15 s par.
- Hackathorn Half Cup stage 6: 10 yd, 2 to slide lock, reload, 2, 7.0 s.

## 8. Headshot at 7 yd

- Gabe White "Immediate Incapacitation": 7 yd, draw, 2 shots to head (4" circle or 3x5 card): Turbo 2.00 s, Light 2.50 s, Dark 3.00 s.
- Gunsite 250 drill: 3 yd single head shot from holster, 1.5 s (x2).
- Hackathorn Half Cup: 3 yd SHO head 2.0 s; 5 yd head 2.0 s; 10 yd head 3.0 s; head zone 3" circle.
- Hackathorn Standards string 1: 5 yd, draw, one head each on 3 targets, 3 s.
- Hackathorn "Wizard" (KR compilation): IDPA 4" head / 8" body, strings at 3/5/7/10 yd, 2.5 s par each, 5 rounds (3 head, 2 body), pass/fail — no over-par and no missed head shots.
- KR Three Seconds or Less string 2: 3 yd, hand on gun, draw, 2 head shots in 3 s; string 6: 7 yd from ready, 2 head shots in 3 s.
- Ben Stoeger "The Heads": draw, 2 rounds per head box at 5/15/25 yd; 25 yd par 6 s; "All As is certainly appropriate" at 5 yd; at 15 yd "You may have a tendency to loosen up your grip ... Usually that leads to not returning the gun back down to the target."
- Spencer Keepers' Test (KR compilation): 3x5 card horizontal, 7 yd, 3 rounds, 3.0 s "salty shooter," 2.5 s "really damn good."
- Tom Givens 5/5/5 (KR compilation): 3x5 card vertical, 5 yd, 5 rounds, 5 s par; ready goal 2.0–2.5 s, holster goal 3.0–3.5 s.

## 9. "10-10-10" / The Test (Ken Hackathorn)

**Origin**: Ken Hackathorn; Larry Vickers ("my good friend and mentor Ken Hackathorn ... originally provided this drill") popularized it with an alternate time-plus scoring.

**Procedure**
- NRA B-8 repair center (5.5" black), 10 yards, 10 rounds, 10 seconds. Start: ready position (Vickers: "trigger finger straight ready position"; Everyday Marksman: 45°/low ready). 15 s allowance for low-capacity pistols is a common variant (secondary).
- Variants (KR compilation): from holster; 1911 version 8 rounds/10 s; "Half & Half" 5 yd, 10 rounds, 5 s.

**Standards**
- Hackathorn point scoring (KR compilation; Everyday Marksman concurs): 80 = pass, 90 = good, 95+ = excellent. **Flag**: many secondary sources state 90 as "pass"; the KR compilation and Everyday Marksman both give 80 pass / 90 good / 95 excellent. Use 90 as the "good" tier.
- Vickers version (AmmoLand, Vickers' own words): "If you're under 10 seconds you pass – if you're over you fail"; shots outside the black but on the B-8 paper +1 s; on cardboard off paper +3 s.
- Kyle Defoor Test 3 (harder variant): B-8 at 25 yd, concealed draw, 10 rounds < 10 s, score 90 or better.
- KR Training student data: 87–100 points at 10 yd.

**What it trains (as stated)**: Everyday Marksman: "ability to manage their handgun sights, grip, and recoil ... balance accuracy against speed over multiple shots." Vickers: fundamental marksmanship under time pressure.

**Scoring**: points (B-8 rings, misses 0); or Vickers time-plus; or over-time = fail.

## 10. Dot Torture

**Origin**: David Blinder (personaldefensetraining.com), popularized by pistol-training.com; page notes "IPSC shooters have told me they were doing this test in the early 90's."

**Procedure (pistol-training.com)**
- 10 dots (2" circles on the printable target), 3 yards, 50 rounds, untimed.
- Dot 1: draw, 5 rounds slow fire best group. Dot 2: draw, 1 shot, holster, x5. Dots 3–4: draw, 1 on #3, 1 on #4, holster, x4 (8). Dot 5: draw, 5 rounds strong hand only. Dots 6–7: draw, 2 on #6, 2 on #7, holster, x4 (16). Dot 8: from ready/retention, 5 rounds weak hand only. Dots 9–10: draw, 1 on #9, speed reload, 1 on #10, holster, x3 (6).
- Pass: "You have to get all 50 hits to pass." Progress by moving back or adding time ("try to finish the entire drill in under 5 minutes while maintaining 100% accuracy").
- Author's note: "a very inefficient way to practice anything, as it is a test."

**What it trains**: page does not itemize; **inference**: trigger control at speed-free baseline, SHO/WHO, reload, presentation.

**Scoring**: pass/fail 50/50; distance is the progression variable.

Source: https://pistol-training.com/shooting-drills/dot-torture/

## 11. Casino Drill (Tom Givens / Rangemaster)

**Origin**: Tom Givens, Rangemaster.

**Procedure (secondary sources describing Givens' drill; no Rangemaster primary page found — flag)**
- Target: Discretionary Command Training Target DT-2A/2B/2C (six numbered shapes, ~8" zones). 5 yards. From holster.
- Load 7/7/7 (21 rounds, two reloads forced). Engage shape 1 with 1 round, shape 2 with 2 ... shape 6 with 6, in numerical order. 21 rounds.
- Par: 21 seconds. Scoring: +1 s per miss, +1 s per shot out of sequence / procedural; functionally pass/fail vs 21 s.

**Standards**
- 21 s clean = Givens' par (Rangemaster). KR Training: "22–27 seconds would be expected" for typical students; range 19–32 s; 10.33 s clean run recorded at the 2019 Rangemaster Instructor Reunion (Uncle Zo).

**What it trains (as stated by secondary sources)**: drawstroke, multi-round string accuracy, emergency reloads, and above all "thinking with a gun in your hand"/processing information under cognitive load.

## 12. 1-5 Drill

- **Attribution flag**: the "1-5 drill" (three targets, 1-2-3-4-5 rounds) is Kyle Lamb's VTAC drill, not Kyle Defoor's. No Defoor "1-5" appears on Defoor's published pistol or carbine test pages.
- VTAC 1-5 (rifle, as published by AmmoLand describing Lamb's drill): three targets ~1 yd apart at 5 yd, start low ready; 1 on T1, 2 on T2, 3 on T3, 4 on T2, 5 on T1 = 15 rounds; goal "all hits in the scoring zone in under five seconds"; hits must be in USPSA A-zone / VTAC square. Stated purpose: "drive the rifle between targets, manage recoil through longer strings of fire, and keep rounds inside the scoring zone."
- **UNVERIFIED**: a Lamb/VTAC primary page with the par; the 5 s goal is from a secondary write-up attributing it to Lamb.
- Defoor's closest published drills: "Speed Shooting / interval" drill (6" circle, 5 yd, 6 rounds at 1.0 / 0.5 / 0.25 s intervals then as fast as possible) per Active Response Training (secondary, and the author says he learned it via Ron Avery).

## 13. Weak-hand-only strings

## Diagnosing "accuracy problem vs speed problem" (recognized coaches)

- **Ben Stoeger (Four Aces page)**: "carefully go through the data from the timer and find the 'low hanging fruit'" — split the string into draw, splits, reload and compare each to the goal.
- **Ben Stoeger (Bill Drill page)**: close range = draw/grip/relaxation test; long range = "a trigger control test." Missed grip shows as adjusting on target, slow shooting "fighting sights," or dropped points. Prescribes Doubles drill (predictive pairs) before Bill Drills; adjust grip pressure (strong hand lighter for trigger speed; support hand firmer for control).
- **Ben Stoeger (Blake Drill)**: "If you bring the pistol up to eye level and you see the front sight isn't sitting in the notch, you probably missed your grip." Transition errors: dragging off or sweeping across targets — "Your gun should go to the center of the target and then stop." "Slowing down probably isn't going to be the ticket to improvement."
- **Ben Stoeger (The Heads)**: at 15 yd, loosened grip "leads to not returning the gun back down to the target" (high hits).
- **Joel Park (Stoeger Pro Shop, "Predictive and Reactive Shooting")**: reactive = confirm the sight picture before every shot; predictive = aim once, fire the pair on rhythm. Diagnostic thresholds: follow-up shots faster than 0.3 s mean "you did not see the sight return and consciously make a choice to fire, instead you are shooting to a rhythm"; slower than 0.6 s means "you are over confirming your sight picture." Front-sight/dot fixation → "shots will tend to climb up higher on the target." Read the group: "look at where the majority of the hits are going and for any patterns. Compare any patterns you see to how your hands feel as you are breaking the shot."
- **Stoeger & Park, Practical Shooting Training (per KR Training review)**: "Par times ... should not be considered a pass/fail test. If the par time is 5 seconds, shooting the drill in 5.2, 4.8, 5.1, 4.7, 5.4 means you are pretty much in the range you want to be in." Training vs testing: "you focus on the process by which you produce the score." Rehn on his own use: "I had to break the drill down (draw, splits, reload, transition, etc.) and look at the timing of each part of the drill, to identify which skill needed the most improvement."
- **Karl Rehn, KR Training (Relative Performance Measurement)**: students who make the time but rack up penalties need a harder accuracy standard ("slide to the right"); draw and reload dominate low-round-count strings, so those are where time gains are. Comstock-style scoring recommended for tracking progress.
- **Tom Givens / Rangemaster (via Baseline Assessment & Bullseye Course)**: pure point scoring on B-8 with fixed pars; rounds after par and hits below the 7-ring count as misses (-10). Instructor proficiency 190/200 (Baseline) and 270/300 (Bullseye) — accuracy is the pass metric; time is a fixed ceiling.
- **Todd Green (pistol-training.com)**: Dot Torture is "a test," not practice; pass/fail at 50/50 then move back (accuracy gate before speed). F.A.S.T. tiers are time-based only after clean hits.
- **Gabe White**: converts accuracy into time (+0.25 C, +1.0 D, +2.0 miss) so speed and accuracy are compared on one axis; pin requires 4 of 8 clean-enough runs (consistency, not a single best).
- **Steve Anderson (Refinement and Repetition)**: **UNVERIFIED** — the book's par-time tables were not accessible online; the RecoilSports interview only documents his method (observe draw from multiple angles, fix stance/grip/hand position) and outcomes (sub-second draws, 4 s El Prez).
